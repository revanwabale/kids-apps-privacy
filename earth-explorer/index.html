Earth Explorer: Kids Atlas — Privacy Policy

No ads or tracking

Last updated: 9 September 2026 · applies to version 2.0 and later

The short version. Earth Explorer never asks who your child is. There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no third-party trackers, and we do not build a profile of anyone. Much of the atlas works on your iPad. Online features include maps, imagery, purchase verification, the narrator's voice, which fetches spoken versions of the app's own sentences, and Ask Kore, an optional feature switched off until a grown-up turns it on — and even then your child's questions are never stored, by us or on the device. These services are described below.

Version 2.0 added features that need the internet — flying down to real places, sharper satellite imagery, yesterday's real clouds, and the optional Ask Kore guide. This policy replaces the earlier one, which described the fully offline version 1.

What stays on your iPad

All of this is stored only on the device, is never uploaded, and is deleted with the app:

  • Passport stamps, quiz scores and streaks.
  • Settings: chosen level, difficulty, whether narration is on.
  • How many Ask Kore questions have been used today, so the daily limit can be applied.
  • Narration audio the app has downloaded, so the same sentence is never fetched twice.
  • For the "How it's going" page for grown-ups: the place, a single word for the subject (for example "animals"), and the date. The question your child asked is never written down. That page is put together on your iPad and is never sent anywhere. It can be erased in one tap.

What leaves your iPad — Kore's narrator voice

The app reads place names and facts aloud in a natural voice. Most of those lines are recorded and shipped inside the app. For eligible original Pass owners and subscribers, when the app needs to speak a line it has no recording for, it sends that line of the app's own text — for example "Brazil holds most of the giant Amazon Rainforest inside its borders" — to our server to be turned into speech, and keeps the audio on the iPad so it is never fetched again. This is the app's own writing, not anything your child said or did, and it happens whether or not Ask Kore is switched on. Turning off "Natural narrator voice" in Settings stops it entirely; the app then uses the voice built into iPadOS. New sample Pass owners use bundled or on-device narration outside their sample answers.

What leaves your iPad — only when Ask Kore is used

Ask Kore is off by default and can only be switched on by a grown-up who passes an arithmetic gate. When a child asks a question, the following is sent to our server and on to Google's Vertex AI so that an answer can be generated:

  • The text of the question.
  • The name of the place on screen (for example "Kenya"), so the answer makes sense.
  • Up to two earlier question-and-answer pairs from the same sitting, so follow-up questions work.
  • A random identifier generated on the device, used only to stop any single copy of the app making an unreasonable number of requests. It is not connected to a name, email, Apple ID or any other identifier, and tells us nothing about who is using the app.

We do not keep the questions or the answers. They pass through our server in order to be answered.

The microphone

If your child speaks a question, the app uses the microphone. The recording is turned into text on the iPad using Apple's on-device speech recognition. Where a device cannot do that on-device, the app offers typing instead rather than sending the recording anywhere. Audio is never stored and never sent to us. iOS will ask your permission before the microphone is used, and the feature works only while Ask Kore is switched on.

Artificial intelligence

Answers from Kore are generated by Google's Gemini model running on Google Cloud Vertex AI. Under the Google Cloud Service Specific Terms, content sent to Vertex AI is not used to train Google's models. Google may retain content for a limited period for abuse monitoring. We deliberately chose Vertex AI over the consumer Gemini service because of this difference in data handling.

We do not build, train or host the model, and we cannot inspect or change how it produces a particular answer. Google's Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy also governs what the service may be used for. If Google changes either of these, the change applies to Ask Kore too and we will update this page.

Answers are produced by a machine, are not reviewed by a person before your child sees them, and can occasionally be wrong. Please see the Terms of Use.

Maps, imagery and the internet

Flying down to a place uses Apple Maps. The sharper ground you see when zooming in, and the "today's clouds" layer, come from NASA's public satellite imagery. Our server runs on Cloudflare. Requests to Apple, NASA and Cloudflare are subject to their own privacy practices; we send them nothing about your child. Without an internet connection the globe, the levels, the quiz, the passport and the narration all still work.

Purchases

Purchases are handled entirely by Apple. We never see card numbers, billing details or your Apple ID — only Apple's confirmation that a purchase is valid. Starting with version 2.0.1 build 14, the new Explorer Pass includes five one-time Ask Kore samples. The app sends Apple's signed purchase transaction to our Cloudflare server to verify eligibility. The server keeps a hash of the original purchase identifier, sample-use counters, request identifiers and answer/voice hashes. It does not persist the signed transaction, question text, answer text or audio in this ledger. Purchase records are used only to provide the feature and enforce the purchased allowance, never for advertising or tracking. They are not sent to Google. The allowance record remains for the lifetime of the purchase so restoring or reinstalling cannot reset it. Contact us about access or deletion; deleting local app data does not delete this server record or cancel purchases.

Children's privacy

Earth Explorer is made for children and is built to collect as little as possible. We do not ask children for names, contact details or profiles. We process the limited question, technical and purchase data described above to provide the app. A child cannot enter their name, create a profile, message anyone, or see content from other users. Purchases and the Ask Kore switch both sit behind a gate intended for grown-ups. We believe this is consistent with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and Apple's Kids Category requirements.

Your choices

  • Leave Ask Kore switched off, and none of your child's words ever leave the iPad.
  • Switch off "Natural narrator voice" in Settings as well, and the app makes no voice requests at all.
  • Erase the curiosity history any time: Settings → Grown-up Zone → How it's going → Erase this history.
  • Reset everything from Settings → Grown-up Zone, or delete the app.
